## Core recommendation

Keep the 1991 photograph itself as an **untouched, locked image layer** in every mode. Style the mat, border, background, and captions around it. Do not colorize, redraw, relight, sharpen, retouch, or add effects over faces. Preserve the full group and original aspect ratio; let people zoom in on phones.

The face-risk ratings below assume each effect is applied to the photo itself. A restrained frame-only version of most styles has low face risk.

## Critique of all eight

| Option | Critical assessment |
|---|---|
| **1. Mechanical Blueprint / Cyanotype** | Strongest connection to Mechanical Engineering and easy to make distinctive. A blue tint over faces could reduce recognition and make the photo feel cold. Keep the photograph unchanged; put drafting lines and technical type on the surrounding mat. |
| **2. 1991 Kodachrome / 35mm Film** | The era cue is appealing, but “Kodachrome” implies color that this black-and-white photo does not contain. AI colorization could invent skin, clothing, and background colors. Use a film-frame or contact-sheet design around the original instead. |
| **3. Sepia & Silver Gelatin** | Archival presentation suits a class yearbook and the original print. The proposed “19th-century” look is a poor period match; heavy sepia can also obscure facial detail. Favor faithful monochrome, a restrained paper mat, and the existing mount. |
| **4. Renaissance Charcoal & Gouache** | Could look like a keepsake, but hatching and painted texture redraw the details people use to recognize one another. It also risks making senior faculty look like caricatures. Keep any art texture outside the photograph. |
| **5. Retro Bollywood Poster** | The Indian cinema reference offers the most obvious playful share-card potential. Hand-painted faces would violate the fidelity requirement, and a crowded poster could turn a reunion photo into a parody. Use a celebratory title banner around the untouched photo. |
| **6. Comic / Halftone Pop Art** | High novelty, but dots and ink outlines simplify eyes, mouths, glasses, and expressions. It is likely to feel like a meme treatment and age poorly as the main yearbook view. At most, use comic-style typography outside the photo. |
| **7. CAD Wireframe / Engineer HUD** | Relevant to the graduates’ field, but contour lines over people can hide features and make the page look like software rather than a yearbook. A restrained drafting-grid frame can work; dense HUD marks will be hard to read on phones. |
| **8. “Class of 2091” Cyberpunk** | The centenary idea is imaginative and could catch attention in a WhatsApp group. Neon lighting changes how faces read, and the sci-fi mood has little connection to the actual 1991 photograph. Keep it as a title-card concept only, if used at all. |

## Definitive TOP 5

1. **Silver-gelatin archive — best default.** It honors the original black-and-white photograph and feels right for a 35th-year reunion. Use the photo as scanned, with a calm paper mount and clear class identification. **Avoid:** heavy sepia, fake age damage, simulated deckled edges over the image, and the “19th-century” framing.

2. **Mechanical blueprint — best class-specific alternate.** It connects directly to the graduates’ discipline and can look polished without changing anyone’s appearance. **Avoid:** blue-tinting the faces, dimension lines through heads, dense labels, or tiny technical type.

3. **1991 film contact sheet — best nostalgia alternate.** Film borders and restrained frame details evoke the period while keeping the monochrome image honest. **Avoid:** claiming the image is color film, AI colorization, strong grain over faces, or scratches that obscure detail.

4. **Bollywood title-card — best celebratory share card.** A bold reunion banner can feel locally familiar and fun for batch sharing while leaving the full group photo intact. **Avoid:** painted or beautified faces, dramatic lighting effects, crowded graphics, and jokes that diminish faculty or classmates.

5. **CAD drafting frame — best technical novelty.** A sparse grid and instrument-style labels can reward the engineering audience without overwhelming the image. **Avoid:** vector contours on people, crosshairs near faces, dense interface readouts, and low-contrast graphics that disappear on phones.

I would leave **charcoal/gouache, comic halftone, and cyberpunk** out of the five featured modes. Their defining effects depend too much on changing or obscuring the people in the photograph.

For WhatsApp, keep the full 67-person photo visible in a landscape card, with a short, readable caption outside it. Don’t crop the group to fit a square preview; provide zoom for anyone who wants to inspect individual faces.
